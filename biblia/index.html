De dónde venimos

La historia de la casa

«Viajo mucho, y en cada país la comida es mi forma de entender a su gente. Siempre soñé con dedicarme a la crítica gastronómica: probar, analizar, descubrir por qué un plato sabe a lo que sabe.

La primera vez que llegué a Colombia sentí algo familiar. La música, los colores, la calidez de la gente, hasta algunos platos me llevaban de vuelta a mi Haití.

Solo faltaba algo: el sabor del Caribe de verdad, el que convierte una comida en un recuerdo. Ahí nació la idea.»

En palabras de quien fundó Azul Caribe Lounge.

4

La idea

«Colombia tiene el espíritu del Caribe, pero aún no ha probado sus verdaderas raíces.»

De esa frase nació Azul Caribe Lounge: traer a Colombia el sabor caribeño de origen, servido con elegancia y sin perder su autenticidad.

5

Tres socios, tres mundos

Cocinar, cocina cualquiera

«Para hacerlo realidad me uní a socios haitianos, colombianos y canadienses. Queríamos un lugar donde las tres culturas se sentaran a la misma mesa y cocinaran el Caribe como se debe: auténtico y moderno.

Cocinar, cocina cualquiera. La diferencia está en la preparación: la marinada, el tiempo, la paciencia y el respeto por cada ingrediente. Por eso, cuando pruebas la carne en Azul Caribe, se nota. Sabe a Caribe. Sabe a casa.»

7

Un lazo viejo

Cartagena y Haití se conocen desde 1815

Ese año, después del sitio de Pablo Morillo, Cartagena cayó en manos españolas. Muchos de sus defensores huyeron por mar y llegaron a Haití, la primera república negra del mundo, independiente desde 1804.

En 1816 el presidente haitiano Alexandre Pétion le dio a Simón Bolívar barcos, armas y hombres para volver a Tierra Firme. Pidió una sola cosa a cambio: que liberara a los esclavizados.

Poner un plato haitiano en una mesa de Cartagena es devolver una visita de doscientos años.

Where we come from

The story of the house

"I travel a lot, and in every country food is how I come to understand its people. I always dreamed of being a food critic: tasting, analyzing, finding out why a dish tastes the way it does.

The first time I came to Colombia I felt something familiar. The music, the colors, the warmth of the people, even some of the dishes took me back to my Haiti.

Only one thing was missing: the true taste of the Caribbean, the kind that turns a meal into a memory. That is where the idea was born."

In the words of the founder of Azul Caribe Lounge.

4

The idea

"Colombia has the spirit of the Caribbean, but it has yet to taste its true roots."

That sentence gave birth to Azul Caribe Lounge: bringing the original taste of the Caribbean to Colombia, served with elegance and without losing its authenticity.

5

Three partners, three worlds

Anyone can cook

"To make it real I joined forces with Haitian, Colombian and Canadian partners. We wanted a place where the three cultures would sit at the same table and cook Caribbean the way it should be: authentic and modern.

Anyone can cook. The difference is in the preparation: the marinade, the time, the patience and the respect for every ingredient. That is why, when you taste the meat at Azul Caribe, you take notice. It tastes like the Caribbean. It tastes like home."

An old bond

Cartagena and Haiti have known each other since 1815

That year, after Pablo Morillo's siege, Cartagena fell to Spain. Many of its defenders fled by sea to Haiti, the world's first Black republic, independent since 1804.

In 1816 Haitian president Alexandre Pétion gave Simón Bolívar ships, weapons and men to return to the mainland. He asked for just one thing in return: to free the enslaved.

Serving a Haitian dish at a Cartagena table is returning a visit two hundred years old.

Guests on the menu

Peru has a seat too

Mixed ceviche · COP 40,000
Fish and seafood cured in lime. It is Peru's national dish, and in 2023 UNESCO listed its preparation as intangible cultural heritage.
Lomo saltado · COP 32,500
Beef stir-fried in a wok with onion, tomato and fries. It was born in chifa cooking, brought by the Chinese immigrants who reached Peru from 1849 on.
The Nikkei touch
Japanese-Peruvian cooking began with the immigrants who arrived in 1899. That is where the love of fresh fish and precise cuts comes from.
